MODULE 1.2 – TIER 1.2 DISASTER PREPAREDNESS AND RESPONSE

Church-Based Disaster Preparedness and Response | BEREDi Network

Module Overview

This module serves as the bridge between preparedness and response. It prepares local churches, families, and volunteers to move from being ready to being responsive when disaster strikes. While Module 1.1 focused on foundational understanding and awareness, Module 1.2 moves participants toward action and mobilization within their communities and church networks.

It emphasizes the strength of the local church as a refuge and ministry center, the power of volunteerism, and the unity of the church network that amplifies response capacity. Through this module, participants will:

  • Recognize the local church’s unique role in both physical and spiritual preparedness.
  • Understand how every believer, family, and ministry group can take part in disaster readiness.
  • Learn to build connections with neighboring churches and community partners for coordinated action.
  • Strengthen their faith-based motivation to serve, knowing that disaster response is both a ministry and a mission.

Audience: This module is designed for local church leaders, volunteers, youth groups, and core teams involved in community-based disaster preparedness and response. It encourages inclusive participation from all church sectors — pastoral, youth, women, and outreach ministries — to build a united readiness culture.

Duration: 3–4 hours (can be delivered as a half-day training or integrated into Tier 1 or Tier 2 sessions for flexible scheduling and reinforcement).

Goal: To equip churches with the knowledge, coordination skills, and faith-driven motivation to serve as active partners in community disaster preparedness and response through unity, volunteerism, and network collaboration.

“Two are better than one, because they have a good return for their labor: If either of them falls down, one can help the other up.” — Ecclesiastes 4:9–10

As the preparatory step for Module 2: Disaster Response, this training helps churches assess readiness, organize volunteers, and connect with the wider network. Together, we become a Church Ready Network — equipped, united, and driven by compassion to respond in times of need.

Lesson 1: The Role of the Local Church

1.1. Church as a Refuge
The church can open its doors for evacuation, feeding, and counseling. Like Noah’s ark, it becomes a place of safety and salvation.

1.2. Organizing Supplies
Encourage each congregation to prepare:

  • First aid kits
  • Nonperishable goods
  • Sleeping mats and blankets

1.3. Offering Spiritual Support
In times of crisis, emotional and spiritual care are as vital as food. Church volunteers can:

  • Pray with survivors
  • Offer counseling
  • Share the Gospel through action

Reflection Question: How can your church show the love of Christ when fear and loss fill the community?

Lesson 2: Volunteerism at the Local Level

2.1. Mobilizing Youth and Families
Youth and family groups can be trained as first responders — assisting in relief distribution, first aid, and information dissemination.

2.2. Serving the Vulnerable
Preparedness must prioritize:

  • Children
  • Elderly
  • Persons with disabilities

“Whatever you did for one of the least of these brothers and sisters of mine, you did for Me.” — Matthew 25:40

2.3. Modeling Christ’s Compassion
Volunteering in disaster response is a ministry of presence — showing the world that God’s people care. It turns compassion into action.

Lesson 3: The Strength of the Church Network

3.1. Unity in Diversity
Each church may differ in size, resources, and culture, but together they form one body in Christ. When disasters strike, networked churches can respond faster and stronger than any single congregation.

“Just as our bodies have many parts and each part has a special function, so it is with Christ’s body.” — Romans 12:4–5

3.2. Shared Resources and Responsibilities
The network enables:

  • Resource sharing — food, volunteers, vehicles, and spaces
  • Information exchange — hazard alerts, needs, and updates
  • Mutual support — prayer, encouragement, and coordination across districts or provinces

3.3. Collaboration with Purpose
