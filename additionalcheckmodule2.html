Lesson 1

Biblical Foundation: The Good Samaritan (Luke 10:25–37)

The parable moves love from words to action. Jesus ends with a commission: “Go and do likewise.”

The story shows compassion that is immediate, personal, and sacrificial: the Samaritan stopped, treated wounds, sheltered the victim, and paid for continued care. Disaster response flows from this same posture—quick, tangible love in times of crisis.

  • Compassion → Action: help the wounded, feed the hungry, comfort the fearful.
  • Mandate for all believers: every member is a “first responder of faith.”
  • Obedience and witness: humanitarian service is a spiritual act of worship.
Reflect: Who is your “neighbor” in current crises? What does “do likewise” look like for your church?

Lesson 2

Practical Immediate Response

Early hours are critical. A ready church becomes a visible expression of Christ’s care.

Provide Essentials

  • Food & Water: ready-to-eat packs, bulk partnerships.
  • Clothing: pre-sorted by size for rapid distribution.
  • Shelter: convert halls/classrooms; designate family areas.
  • Simple Medical Care: first aid teams; link with barangay health workers.

Mobilize Volunteers

  • Form teams: preparation, cleanup, logistics, transport, comms.
  • Appoint team leads reporting to a response coordinator.
  • Coordinate with LGUs/CSOs; issue safety briefs, gloves, masks, tools.

Open Church Facilities as Safe Shelters

  • Pre-plan space, signage, hygiene and sleeping areas, prayer zone.
  • Stock mats, blankets, flashlights, hygiene kits; maintain order/cleanliness.
Preparedness is an act of love. Every pack and every presence says, “God has not forgotten you.”
